Preparing for your operation

Patients often ask how they should prepare. For most people there is nothing special to do, but three things help.

Keep active. The fitter you are before surgery, the more easily you recover. A brisk walk every day, enough to leave you a little out of breath, is ideal. Do a little more each week.

Eat well. Regular, balanced meals with plenty of protein help your body to heal. If you have lost weight without meaning to, or your appetite is poor, please let us know.

Check your medicines. Some medicines may need to be paused before an operation. The most common are:

  • antiplatelet medicines, such as aspirin and clopidogrel, often taken after a heart attack, a stroke or a stent;
  • anticoagulants (blood thinners), such as warfarin, apixaban and rivaroxaban, often taken for an irregular heartbeat or a previous blood clot;
  • medicines such as dapagliflozin and empagliflozin, taken for diabetes, heart failure or kidney disease.

If you take any of these, please check with us before your operation. We will tell you whether to pause it, and when. Please do not stop anything on your own.

Coming into hospital

You will usually be admitted at 7am on the day of surgery.

Please do not eat anything from midnight. Drink a glass of water every hour until your surgery.

I will come to see you before your operation, to go through the plan and answer any remaining questions.

Day of surgery

When we call you to theatre, an anaesthetist will meet you and look after you throughout, so that you are asleep and comfortable for the whole operation. Before you know it, you will be awake in the recovery area.

We aim for you to be free of pain from that moment, so if you are uncomfortable please tell the nurse looking after you.

If you wake with a small chest drain, we remove it as soon as it is safe to do so.

You will usually spend a few hours in the recovery area, and then go to the ward.

Going home

We plan for you to go home on the same day. Please do not worry: you will not be discharged if there is any clinical concern, or if you do not feel confident to go.

Before you leave, we make sure you are free of pain and well enough to go.

It takes a little time to get your medicines from the pharmacy, so please allow for this when you arrange your journey home.
