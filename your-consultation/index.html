Your referral

An NHS consultation begins with a referral from your hospital’s multidisciplinary team. Once we receive it, we process the information and send you an appointment letter.

By the time we meet, I will already have reserved a provisional operation date for you in the first available slot. If you decide to go ahead, you do not then have to wait for a date to be found, so your treatment can start sooner. If you decide against surgery, the date is simply released.

Private patients

Private patients are self-funding and can make an appointment directly.

I regret that I no longer work with insurance companies. It keeps my practice small enough to give each patient my full attention, and it means that decisions about your care are made by you and me, with no outside authorisation to wait for.

My office can give you a quote for your consultation, and for any treatment, before you book. To make an appointment, call +44 (0) 207 352 8121 or send a message.

Before we meet

If time permits, we send you an invitation letter and a health questionnaire. Completing it in advance, including the medicines you take and their doses, means our time together can be spent on discussion.

Clinics are held at the Royal Brompton Hospital. It helps if you can bring your completed self-assessment leaflet. If you have not had the chance to complete it, a list of your medicines and, ideally, of your past illnesses is a great help.

Who will be in the clinic

NHS clinics can be busy. The people usually present are Maria, my practice manager, who helps with administration and admission; a clinical nurse specialist, who is there to support you; and a research nurse, who can tell you about any research you may be able to take part in. You can meet the team before you come.

Surgeons from around the world apply to spend time with me, to learn the techniques I have developed and take them home to their own patients. Medical students join us too, as the Royal Brompton is a teaching hospital. When you allow them to sit in on your consultation, you help patients far beyond this clinic, and I am grateful to everyone who does.

If you would prefer visiting doctors and students not to be present, please tell Maria and we will make sure they are not. This will not affect your consultation or your care in any way.

During the consultation

We start with your health: the better I understand you and your physical health, the more closely my recommendations can be tailored to you. We then go through your condition and discuss the options together. Your questions are always welcome.

Your clinic letter and consent

Once we have decided together on the best course, I will offer you a date for your operation, where that applies.

You leave the appointment with your clinic letter in your hand. It sets out the details of your operation and its risks. In many clinics the letter follows by post, days or weeks later. Here it is ready before you leave, so you do not have to wait for it. You can read it again that evening, talk it through with your family, and show it to your GP or your other doctors while our conversation is still fresh.

Consent is a process, and it happens in two stages so that you have time to digest the information from clinic. Your clinic letter is the first stage: it records what we have discussed and agreed. The consent form is the second stage, and you are usually asked to sign it when you are admitted for your operation.
