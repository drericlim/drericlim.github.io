One small incision

Most of my operations are now performed through a single keyhole incision no longer than three finger breadths, about 3 to 5 cm. We start with an incision of two finger breadths, and gentle retraction of the soft tissue makes the end result slightly larger. This is now my standard approach for major lung resections.

A lot can be done through one keyhole. The majority of my operations are performed this way, including pleural biopsy, lung biopsy, surgery for pleural effusion and pneumothorax, and major lung resections.

Patients often wonder how so much can be done through such a small incision. This film shows how I perform a lung resection through a single keyhole.

How we got here

When I started independent practice, the vast majority of my operations were performed through an open incision between the ribs, called a thoracotomy. With time this was replaced by three keyholes, and now by one.

In 2008 the average stay in hospital after a lung resection was 7 days. By 2015 it was 2 days. Now, many are home on the same day.

No single change explains this. Better skills, better equipment, better pain control, better care after the operation and a better team approach have each played a part.

The evidence: the VIOLET trial

For many years nobody knew for certain whether keyhole surgery for lung cancer was better than open surgery, because the two had never been compared in a large trial where chance alone decided which operation each patient had. I conceived and led VIOLET to answer that question.

Trials like this are rare in surgery because they are hard to set up, run and complete. VIOLET was funded by the National Institute for Health Research and ran in nine NHS hospitals. Between 2015 and 2019, 503 patients agreed to take part.

VIOLET showed that patients who had keyhole surgery recovered better. They had less pain, fewer complications, a shorter stay in hospital and a better quality of life over the following year, with no compromise to the treatment of their cancer.1,2

We then looked within the trial at keyhole operations done through a single incision and those done through several. Pain in hospital was similar. After going home, patients who had a single incision reported less pain over the first three months, and better physical function for up to a year.3

Going home

We do not send you home early to meet a target. We work to get you free of pain and well enough that you can choose to go home when you are ready.

By the morning after surgery we aim for you to have “no pain and no drain”. We do not achieve this in every patient. Now, many are home on the same day.

Seeing our patients out in the park the day after a major lung resection is what drives us to achieve this consistently.

When keyhole surgery is not suitable

Not everyone is suitable for keyhole surgery. If a cancer is central and a major airway or blood vessel may need to be reconstructed, the operation is done through an open incision.

I will also change to an open operation if the keyhole approach is not technically possible, or if I meet any difficulty during surgery, so that your operation can continue in the safest way.
